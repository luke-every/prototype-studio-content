      How the recommendation works

      One page for stakeholders. Spec: Notion "Recommendation Logic – [V2] NC Onboarding Quiz", sections 2.1–2.7.

      Step 1 · 2.1Filter

      Remove every meal that contains an allergen the customer selected. Never relaxed.

      Step 2 · 2.2Score

      Rank the remaining meals on goal fit, reviews, spice, cuisine and nutrition.

      Step 3 · 2.3–2.4Fill the box

      Take the top meals: 15 for one person, 20 for bigger households.

      Step 4 · 2.4Add breakfast + shot

      One breakfast product and one Ritual Cubes pack, picked by goal.

      Step 5 · 2.5Suggest add-ons

      Up to 3 optional add-ons in a separate section. Not part of the box.

      Core rules

        Add-on rules

        • Shown in a separate section under the box. The customer opts in.
        • Max 3 slots: goal pick, preference pick, treat pick. An empty slot is simply skipped.
        • Add-ons never count toward the quantity tier and never toward the 15/20 meals.
        • Same allergen filter as meals. Low sugar or diabetes removes anything over 5 g sugar per serving and all treats.
        • No duplicates: an add-on is never the breakfast or shot already in the box.

        UX/UI rules for the recommendation page

        What design and front-end need to build. Rules only; numbers, tables and the full ranking logic live in the spec and the other tabs.

        Recommendation Logic – [V2] NC Onboarding Quiz (full logic) Claude Box Simulator Product mapping table (add-on mapping)

        1Core recommendation

        • Size follows "How many people will eat every week?" Just me: 15 meals. Partner, family or roommates: 20 meals.
        • Every box also has 1 breakfast product and 1 Ritual Cubes shot pack, picked by goal. They sit inside the box, not in the add-on section.
        • One primary CTA adds the whole core recommendation to the cart (meals + breakfast + shot). No other CTA on the page adds the box.
        • One card per meal, in rank order. A meal that appears twice shows a ×2 badge, never two cards.
        • Show 4 swap options below the box. They aren't in the box and don't change the total. Hide the row if there are no eligible meals left.
        • Tapping a meal opens a swap sheet with eligible meals ranked by score. Meals excluded by allergy never appear there.
        • The customer can swap, remove and change quantities. The CTA is disabled under 15 meals, with the reason shown.
        • Price block: total, price per meal, "Minimum 15 meals", and "skip, pause or cancel anytime".
        • Frame it as a suggestion: "Your suggested box", with "Edit my box" and "Browse all meals" next to the main CTA.
        • Same answers always give the same box.

        2Personalised add-ons

        • A separate section under the box, titled around the customer's answers (e.g. "Picked for your goals").
        • Each add-on is selectable on its own and added to the cart separately. Selecting or removing one never changes the box, its price or its quantity tier.
        • Max 3 slots: goal pick, preference pick, treat pick. Empty slots are hidden; if all are empty, hide the section.
        • Examples (full mapping in the product mapping table):
          • Fitness or "Getting more protein" → 1 Boonian Crumbles + 1 Planted. Pulled (2 packs)
          • Likes medium or very spicy food → Chili Crisp Oil
          • Family with kids → Épique 9 Bites Box (raw cakes); smoothie 3-pack as backup
          • After having a baby or gut health → porridge + a smoothie 3-pack
        • Same allergen filter as the meals. An add-on with a selected allergen is never shown.
        • "Lowering my sugar intake" or "Diabetes-friendly" hides anything over 5 g sugar per serving and all treats.
        • Never repeat a product that's already in the box (e.g. the breakfast or shot).

        3Optional upsell row To explore

        • A second row with an offer, e.g. "Try these 4 meals for just €10 extra".
        • Each upsell item is independently selectable. The core recommendation CTA stays separate and unchanged.
        • Suggested source: the next-ranked meals (the 4 swap options), so the offer is still personalised and allergen-safe.
        • To decide before design: does the €10 offer count toward the quantity tier, can it combine with the first-order discount, and does it replace or sit next to the swap row? €2.50 per meal is close to meal COGS (about €1.90–2.70), so Finance should sign off.

        4Explaining the recommendation

        • Explain with the signals that ranked the box, strongest first: goal fit, customer ratings, spice match, favourite cuisine, nutrition answers.
        • One summary line for the box, built from the answers, e.g. "Picked for your fitness goal, with Indian dishes and mild spice."
        • Up to 2 reason tags per meal, measurable facts only: "25 g protein", "12 g fibre", "Not spicy", "Italian". Optional "Best match" when the meal meets every criterion of the goal.
        • Only tag what was asked. No cuisine tag if no cuisine was chosen or none matched; no tag from missing data.
        • Never imply every meal meets the goal, and never promise a health result.
        Say"25 g protein" · "Picked for your goal" · "High in fibre"
        Don't say"Weight-loss meals" · "Gut-friendly box" · "Diabetes-friendly"

        5Allergen messaging

        • What we do: leave out meals and add-ons that contain a selected allergen. The quiz asks about gluten, soy, mustard, tree nuts, sesame and celery.
        • What we don't do: exclude products that may contain traces of an allergen, particularly nuts. Peanuts aren't asked yet.
        • So never claim the box is allergen-free, safe for allergies, or free from traces.
        • Show the note whenever an allergy was selected, near the box and again in the cart.
        Say"We've left out meals that contain [allergens]. Some products may contain traces of allergens, including nuts. Please check the ingredients of each product."
        Don't say"Your allergen-free box" · "Safe for nut allergies" · "No traces of gluten"
